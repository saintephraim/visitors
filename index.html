A place for you

You are welcome here.

Whether you are discovering Orthodoxy for the first time or looking for a spiritual home, we are glad you found us.

St. Ephraim is a community of Orthodox Christians in north-central San Antonio, rooted in the ancient faith and apostolic tradition of the Church. Our parish brings together people from many backgrounds, united in worship and a desire to grow in Christ.

You do not need to be Orthodox to join us. Come, listen to the prayers, experience the beauty of the Liturgy, and take your time. We would love to meet you.

Your first visit

A little guidance. A warm welcome.

Orthodox worship may feel unfamiliar at first. Here are a few things to help you feel at home.

01Do I need to be Orthodox to visit?

Not at all. Everyone is welcome to attend our services, whether you are exploring Christianity, visiting from another tradition, or simply curious. Come as you are, observe, and feel free to ask questions afterward.

02What should I wear?

Comfortable, modest clothing is appropriate. There is no need to buy anything special for your visit. You will see a range of dress, and comfortable shoes are helpful because much of the service is spent standing.

03Will I need to stand the whole time?

Standing is a traditional posture of prayer in Orthodox worship, but please sit whenever you need to. Your health and comfort matter. You are welcome to participate at your own pace.

04Can I bring my children?

Yes! Children are welcome in the life and worship of the Church. Do not worry if your little ones make a little noise or need a break. Come together as a family.

05May I receive Holy Communion?

Holy Communion is reserved for Orthodox Christians who have prepared to receive it through prayer, fasting, and confession under the guidance of their priest. Visitors are warmly welcome to pray with us. If you have questions about Communion or the blessed bread offered after the service, please speak with the priest.

06Why are there icons, candles, and incense?

Orthodox worship engages all of our senses. Icons remind us of Christ and His saints; candles accompany our prayers; and incense symbolizes our prayers rising to God. You are welcome to observe without feeling that you need to copy every gesture.

07How long is the Divine Liturgy?

Plan for roughly an hour and a half to two hours, though the length varies, especially on feast days. Matins precedes the Divine Liturgy, so prayers may already be underway when you arrive.

08What if I do not know what to do?

You are in good company: many Orthodox Christians first came as visitors themselves. Listen, watch, and let the service unfold. There is no expectation that you will know the prayers or customs on your first visit.

Explore the ancient faith

Questions are a good place to begin.

Orthodoxy is a life to be experienced, as well as a faith to be learned. You are invited to explore, ask questions, and discover the Church at your own pace.

What is Orthodox Christianity?

The Orthodox Church shares the faith, worship, and sacramental life handed down from Christ and His Apostles. At St. Ephraim, we live this ancient Christian tradition as part of the Antiochian Orthodox Christian Archdiocese of North America.

Why is the Church called a hospital for souls?

The Church understands salvation as healing and communion with God. Christ is our physician, and the life of the Church—prayer, repentance, worship, and the sacraments—helps us grow in His love. We come seeking healing together.

How can I learn more about the faith?

Begin by attending services and introducing yourself to the priest. Ask about opportunities for catechism and learning in the parish. You do not need to know everything before you begin, and there is room for your questions.

How does someone become Orthodox?

The journey begins with getting to know the parish and speaking with the priest. A period of learning and preparation, called the catechumenate, leads toward reception into the Church. The priest guides each person according to their circumstances.
